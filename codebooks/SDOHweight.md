## Weight `[W]`

**Definition**: code any time weight is mentioned or discussed between the clinician and patient throughout the encounter. 

**Guidelines**:
- keywords: diet quality, nutrition, food intake, eating, body shape, and body size, current weight, past weight gain during pregnancy
- Dropdown-style：  
    - Was the word “weight” used versus any other way of referring to this topic (e.g., body size, feelings about size or shape)
    - Who initiated weight discussion (patient vs. clinician)
        - Clinician initiated: I see that you have gained a few pounds since your last appointment 
        - Patient initiated: I got on the scale before they brought me back to the exam room and I see that I’ve gained 2 more pounds.” 
    - Clinician discusses a plan or educates the patient on weight gain or (in some cases) weight loss

**Examples**:
- Normal weight expressions:
    - “I know you’ve been doing really well at keeping food down so you are not losing weight.” 
    - “I know you are eating for two. You also should try to eat healthy things as that is better for you baby.”
    - “Drinking plenty of water and engaging in light exercise are healthy ways to gain the right amount of weight.” 
- Drop down: Instances of judgement during weight discussions. Judgement will come from the clinician to the patient. 
    - “I’m guessing you have not found time to exercise right?”
    - “You have already gained a lot of weight. Better slow that down.”
    - “Maybe cut back on the sugary and high fat foods.”

## Social Determinants of Health `[SD]`

**Definition**: code any time social determinants of health that are mentioned throughout the encounter (any of these would count)

**Guidelines and Examples**:
- Finances: 
    - Counts: 
        - Patient: “I can’t really afford that medication right now.” 
        - Either patient or provider: “Baby formula and clothes are a lot more expensive than they used to be.” 
        - Provider: “Are you having any trouble covering costs of taking care of yourself right now?”
    - Do Not Code:
        - “Sometimes they’re covered by insurance”
- Employment/Job
    - Counts: 
        - Patient: “I’m currently not employed.” 
        - Patient: “Only way I can make it is working two jobs.”
        - Either patient or provider: “Working a full-time job can be a lot with two small children.” 
        - Provider: “Are you working?”
- Housing insecurity
    - Counts: 
        - Patient: “I am worried I will be evicted.” 
        - Patient: “I just recently moved back in with my parents.” 
        - Provider: “What is your housing situation?”
- Food insecurity
    - Counts:
        - Patient: “I’m having trouble getting decent food.”
        - Patient: “Things get pretty tight at the end of the month, and I have to be careful about what I am eating.”
        - Provider: “Will you have any trouble getting healthy food like fruits and vegetables?”
- Transportation issues
    - Counts: 
        - Patient: “I couldn’t make it to that ultrasound appointment because my car broke down.”
        - Provider: “I noticed you have missed the last few appointments. Do you need help with anything like transportation?”
- Limited social support (references to support from partner, family, friends, community members, social network)
    - Counts:
        - Provider: “Are you getting any support from the child’s father?”
        - Patient: “I don’t have anyone helping me with this pregnancy.”
- Safety (any references or mention of safety concerns from crime, neighborhood, violence including partner violence, any references to feeling afraid or threatened)
    - Counts:
        - Patient: “We were robbed last week so I’m dealing with a lot right now.”
        - Provider: “Any concerns about your safety?”
