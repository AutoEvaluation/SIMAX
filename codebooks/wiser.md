## Empathic Opportunity (Patient Only) `[EO]`

**Definition**: 
An empathic opportunity is when the patient explicitly or implicitly expresses an emotion. Empathic opportunities are topics brought up by the patient or family member/loved one. 

**Guidelines**:
- Code individually each time an emotion comes up, even if the emotion was mentioned before.
- Only code Empathic Opportunity for negative emotion (not positive)
- Do not code if:
    - The emotion follows a clinician’s empathic response (`[ER]`).
    - The patient is responding to empathy rather than initiating emotion.
- Use:
    - `[EO]` for patient-initiated expressions.
    - `[EOV]` for visitor/loved one-initiated expressions.
- Past emotional experiences also count as valid empathic opportunities.

**Examples**:
- Direct (explicit emotion)
    - “I dread childbirth.”
    - “Now I am scared.”
    - “I have been really depressed lately.” 
    - “I am frustrated with all of these pains and morning sickness.” 
- Indirect (implied emotion)
    - “I just want to make sure you're not missing anything.” 
    - “Why me?” 
    - “I’m not sure how I will handle this.” 
    - “Well I guess it is what it is and there is nothing I can do.”
    - *Sigh…*, *Crying*, *Oh no!* 
- Imperceptible empathic opportunity
    - “Can I get you a tissue?”
- Medical questions that are emotional questions (pay attention to tone, pitch, etc.)
    - “Wait, you are saying my baby is not ok?”
    - “You mean this is hurting my baby?”
- A series of fast-paced, repetitive questions indicate anxiety.
    - Expressions of extreme pain 
    - “This pain in my back is really unbearable.”
    - “I can’t even make it down the stairs without shooting pains.”

## Empathic Response (Clinician Only) `[ER]`

**Definition**: 
A statement in response to an empathic opportunity (Clinician uses an “I wish” statement, names emotion, or otherwise explicitly acknowledges patient or caregiver emotion).

**Guidelines**:
- Wish statement refers to a statement in which the Clinician uses the exact phrase “I wish” in reference to something they do not have control over. The statement must have “I wish” in it to qualify.
- Clinician names emotion refers to an attempt by the Clinican to name an emotion that the patient seems to be experiencing. 
- Only code for negative emotions.
- The attempt is valid even if the patient claims the named emotion is not how they are feeling.
- Clinician acknowledges emotion refers to a statement by the CLINICIAN that recognizes the emotion, or the gravity of the situation, but doesn’t explicitly name it
- Do not code: 
    - “How are you feeling?”
    - “I really want you to see a social worker about this” or any referral to discuss emotional aspects

**Examples**:
- Wish statement:
    - “I wish I could tell you something differently.”
    - “I really wish there was something else we could do.”
    - “I wish quitting were that easy for everyone.”
    - “I wish I had better news about your lab results”
- Clinician names emotion:
    - “Sounds like you’re feeling scared.”
    - “You seem overwhelmed.”
    - "You've been worried about that, huh?"
    - MD: “You seem shocked” PT: “No, I’m actually just worried about my baby”
    - “You look taken aback by what I just said.”
    - “That’s scary huh?”
    - “You seem more worried about how your family will handle this.”
    - “I can see you are sad”
    - “It seems this has upset you so.”
    - “You seem quiet today.”
    - “Tell me more about what your concerns are, tell me more about how you feel.”
- Clinician acknowledges emotion:
    - “This must be hard”
    - “I can’t imagine what you’re going through”
    - “This is such a difficult situation”

## Empathetic Statement Independent Of Oppotunity or Empathic Statement (Clinician Only) `[ES]`

**Definition**: An empathic statement is an acknowledgment of patient or caregiver emotion 

**Guidelines**:
- Only coded independently (i.e. there is no previous empathic opportunity expressed by a patient or caregiver). 
- The Clinician addresses emotion or expresses empathy without a previous empathic opportunity expressed by a patient or caregiver. Count each statement.
- Do not code for general praise, such as “You are doing such a great job”, but if the doctor specifically names something the patient is doing and make it more emotional, such as “You are doing such a great job taking care of your other children”, then this should be coded as an Empathic Statement (or Empathic Response if there was an Empathic Opportunity).

**Examples**:
- “This can be incredibly frustrating”
- “I’m sure you’re anxious to hear the results”
- Clinician: I wish we were just going to talk about something simpler, but I know um that we've communicated about the plan regarding um, how we can help you and the baby.

## I’m Sorry (Clinician Only) `[S]`

**Definition**: A clinician acknowledges emotion or potential emotion by saying “I’m sorry” without addressing the emotion or continuing the conversation

**Guidelines**:
- This does not count as a true empathic statement, but still deserves recognition.

**Examples**:
- “I’m sorry about the wait time today.”
- “Sorry it makes for such a long day when we have to get bloodwork first.”

## Open-ended question (Clinician only) `[OE]`

**Definition**:
- Clinician asks a question that cannot be answered with a yes or no.
- Clinician asks a question that starts with “why”
- Clinician asks a question that cannot be answered with a finite answer 

**Guidelines**:
- Counts: 
    - “What have they told you about the risks to you and your baby?”
    - “What do you think you can do to increase decrease your pain?”
    - “Why do you think that is?”
    - “What has helped you in the past?”
    - “What are your biggest concerns today?”
    - “Tell me more”
- Do not code: 
    - Questions with finite answers, such as: 
        - And when it comes how long does it stay, this discomfort?
        - And how many times a day does that happen?
        - Any closed-ended questions beginning with “are, do, did”
    - Clinician interrupts with their own response before patient can answer the question
    - Clinician follows open-ended with closed question before the patient responds 
- If there is a series of questions in same passage, only code the last one (e.g., do not count three open questions if they are asked in the same passage). 
- If the Clinician asks an open-ended question and follows up with a closed-ended question or vice versa. Then, it will not count as open-ended as the patient will answer the closed-ended question. 

**Examples**:
- “What have they told you about the health risk regarding the baby?” 
- “Did they go over your bloodwork with you?”

## Reflective Statements (Clinician Only) `[RS]`

**Definition**: 
This category is meant to capture reflective listening statements made by the Clinician in response to patient statements and anybody else in the room (e.g., family members/others in the exam room) but not clinic staff. Reflections may introduce new meaning or material, but they essentially capture and return to patients something about what they have just said. 

**Guidelines**:
- The clinician may finish the patient’s sentence 
- The reflective statement does not have to immediately follow what the patient says 
- The clinician reflects on things mentioned in a previous visit 
- The clinician starts the statement with “I remember…” 
- Counts even if the reflective statement is not medical.
- If question is asked within or after the reflective statement, it has to be independent of the statement otherwise it counts as a question (and only coded if it is open)
- Do not code: 
    - If the patient asks a question and the clinician answers 
    - statements starting with “yes” “no” or “exactly” 
    - If the clinician says that they’ve looked in the patient’s chart and repeats something back to them 
    - If the clinician asks a question as a part of a reflective statement
    - If the clinician repeats patient statement while charting.

**Note**:
- Lumping vs. splitting: Reflective statements must be statements and not questions. If they are two separate utterances that reflect two different things separated by unrelated texted, code both reflections. If the reflections constitute a summary and are several reflections in a row, code as one reflection.
- Remember to look for a reflection embedded in a sentence that starts out as a reflection then moves on to ask a separate question. For example, “I heard you say you like to go for walks. Which trails are your favorite?” The first part is still a reflective statement even though the second part is a closed question. This does not pertain to the entire portion of what they are saying as being a question as questions are not reflective statements. For example, “You have not had a good appetite?” is a question, not a reflective statement, whereas, “You have not had a good appetite” is a reflective statement. It only pertains to when there is a reflective statement that precedes a separate question. 
- We do not code for general praise, such as “You are doing such a great job”, but if the clinician specifically names something the patient is doing, such as “You are doing such a great job with your walking”, then this should be coded as a Reflection.
- If the clinician makes an empathic statement, do not code it as a reflection. It is either or, rather than double-coded. 

**Examples**:
- Example 1: 
    - Patient: “They said there’s no sign of birth defects.”
    - Clinician: Exactly, there are no sign of birth defects which is good news.”
- Example 2: 
    - Clinician: How has your nausea been? [OE]
    - Patient: It has been better these days. I still have trouble eating.
    - Clinician: So you are still struggling with eating. [RS]
- Example: Reflection and Question in Sequence: Sometimes the Clinician begins with a reflection, but adds a question to “check” the reliability of the reflection. Just code the reflection unless the question is open.
    - Patient: “I just can’t keep going to the hospital like this.”
    - Clinician: “The hospital is not your favorite place. Is that right?” [RS]

## Elicit questions (Clinician Only) `[EQ]`

**Definition**: Anywhere in the encounter, the Clinician asks, “What questions do you have?” 

**Guidelines**:
- Variations can be: 
    - “What other concerns do you have?”
    - “I know you’ve got a lot of questions” 
    - “I am sure you have questions” does count. 
- Do not code (closed questions)
    - “Do you have any questions?”
    - “Is there anything else?”
    - “Questions for me?” 
    - ”Any questions for me?”
